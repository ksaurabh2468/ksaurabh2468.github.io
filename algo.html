Why this is hard to hire for

Two skills that rarely sit in one person

Plenty of developers can write code. Plenty of traders understand options. The intersection — someone who can hold both a Greeks-aware strategy and a fault-tolerant execution system in their head at once — is genuinely narrow.

A developer alone

Ships something that works on the happy path. Misses that a stalled feed still returns a price, that a retried order can double your position, and that most of your strategy's states never occur on the day they tested it.

A trader alone

Knows exactly what the strategy should do, and has no way to prove the code does it. Ends up supervising an automation manually — which is most of the cost of trading it by hand, with none of the confidence.

What I do

Take a strategy you already trade, turn it into a system that executes it exactly, and prove that it does — with replayable tests over historical ticks, not a week of hoping.

Principles

How I work

This is the part that matters. Anyone can write the entry logic — the difference between an automation you trust and one you babysit is entirely in the discipline around it.

  1. 01

    Your strategy stays yours

    You bring the edge. I build the system that runs it. Your logic isn't reused, isn't shared with anyone else, and isn't traded by me. That's in writing before anything starts.

  2. 02

    Replay before paper, paper before live

    Every rule is first proven against recorded market data, replayed deterministically so the same input always produces the same output. Then live data with simulated orders. Real money is the last step, never the first.

  3. 03

    Every state gets tested, not just the ones that happened

    A strategy with a dozen states won't visit most of them on any given day. Live observation can't verify what it never encounters — so states get constructed and tested deliberately, including the boundaries and the ugly ones.

  4. 04

    It fails loudly, never silently

    A missing price halts the system; it does not quietly substitute the last one it saw. A stopped system costs you an opportunity. A system running on wrong data costs you capital. Those aren't the same risk.

  5. 05

    You get evidence, not assurances

    Every requirement traces to a test, and every test to a recorded result you can inspect. "It works" isn't a deliverable. The proof is.

  6. 06

    Go-live is supervised, not a switch

    First session at reduced size with someone watching and the kill switch armed. Then a staged ramp. Plus an agreed rollback plan written before cutover, not improvised during an incident.
