Why this is hard to hire for

Two skills that rarely sit in one person

Plenty of developers can write code. Plenty of traders understand options. The intersection — someone who can hold both a Greeks-aware strategy and a fault-tolerant execution system in their head at once — is genuinely narrow.

A developer alone

Ships something that works on the happy path. Misses that a stalled feed still returns a price, that a retried order can double your position, and that most of your strategy's states never occur on the day they tested it.

+

A trader alone

Knows exactly what the strategy should do, and has no way to prove the code does it. Ends up supervising an automation manually — which is most of the cost of trading it by hand, with none of the confidence.

What I do

Take a strategy you already trade, turn it into a system that executes it exactly, and prove that it does — with replayable tests over historical ticks, not a week of hoping.

Principles

How I work

This is the part that matters. Anyone can write the entry logic — the difference between an automation you trust and one you babysit is entirely in the discipline around it.

Stage 1

Replay

Every rule proven against recorded market data, replayed deterministically — same input, same output, every time. Every state a strategy can hit is constructed and tested on purpose, not just the ones that happened to occur.

Stage 2

Paper

Live market data, simulated orders. The system behaves exactly as it will in production — the only thing missing is real money on the line.

Stage 3

Live

First session at reduced size, someone watching, kill switch armed. Then a staged ramp, with a rollback plan agreed before cutover — not improvised during an incident.

Evidence, not assurances. Every requirement traces to a test, and every test to a recorded, inspectable result — at every stage above. "It works" isn't a deliverable. The proof is.
